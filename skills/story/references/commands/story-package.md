# Explain the film that actually exists
Read the revised screenplay and current reviews, then use MASTER_PROMPT.md's complete package and stakeholder-pitch requirements. Read development/package_options.json. Explain the available choices only as needed and record the writer's selected sections, screenplay and pitches with scripts/package_options.py. Write only selected content; unrequested templates may remain blank. The full selection has 15 content modules and 16 pitches. Include logline, short/long synopsis and actual ending, treatment, characters, visual/sound language, positioning/comparables, casting rationale, director/producer proposals, production assumptions, festival/awards hypotheses when appropriate, scene showcase, marketing and risks.

Every pitch should answer its reader's decision: actor role, director opportunity, producer assembly, financier assumptions, distributor audience, and so on. Do not repeat the same copy with new headings. Distinguish proposals, estimates, and verified facts; verify current business claims if you use them. Never fabricate attachments, interest, reviews, returns, rights clearance, or awards prospects. Match scenes and chronology to the screenplay and identify material drift. Write development/package_review.md with claim-to-scene checks, source-label consistency, assumptions, actual inventory, and a truthful verdict. A selected subset is a complete package for that selection; omitted writing/research stages stay incomplete when outside scope. Never require an unselected pitch just to pass a gate.

Optional parallel research/review follows TEAMS.md and development/teams.json, off by default. Keep one author and do not expose these settings unless useful or requested.

For a novel, memoir, article, play or other supplied non-screenplay source, read ADAPTATION.md and use story-adapt. Source fidelity, adaptation permission status and factual truth are separate. Recheck current source-to-scene mapping after substantive changes.
