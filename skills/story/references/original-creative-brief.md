# CLAUDE.md

## Project: *The Letter to the Father*  
### Prestige feature screenplay + studio package + LaTeX build system

This file gives Claude a precise operating doctrine for generating, revising, and maintaining the full repository for an Oscar-caliber prestige film project centered on **Adrien Brody as Franz Kafka**.

The goal is not to produce generic screenplay output. The goal is to produce a **dangerously polished, studio-credible, festival-ready, actor-bait screenplay and package** that could plausibly circulate among serious producers, agents, and prestige financiers.

---

## Mission

Build and maintain a complete multi-file repository for a feature film package currently titled:

# **The Letter to the Father**

This repository must contain:

- a full feature screenplay
- a polished studio package
- one-pagers for actor / director / financier outreach
- awards strategy materials
- packaging notes
- LaTeX modules for clean, professional PDF generation
- a build system that compiles deliverables reliably

All writing should feel authored, emotionally coherent, restrained, cinematic, and genuinely usable in the real world.

---

## Core creative concept

### Logline
In the final years of his life, a frail and tormented Franz Kafka struggles to write, love, and exist within a world of family pressure, bureaucratic humiliation, illness, and spiritual dread, while the stories he creates begin to mirror and consume the life he can no longer control.

### Core framing
This is **not** a generic literary biopic.

It is a psychological prestige drama in which Kafka’s real life gradually takes on the emotional and structural logic of a Kafka story, while remaining grounded, elegant, and awards-friendly.

The film should feel like:
- intimate but severe
- literary but cinematic
- historically grounded but not museum-stiff
- psychologically subjective without becoming gimmicky
- actor-forward without becoming stagey
- visually controlled without becoming inert

## Adrien Brody mandate

This project is designed as a prestige starring vehicle for **Adrien Brody**.

Everything should be calibrated around what Brody does unusually well:

- inwardness
- haunted intelligence
- silence
- physical fragility
- emotional implosion
- isolation under pressure
- micro-expression instead of showy rhetoric
- strength expressed as endurance rather than dominance

The screenplay must create:
- at least 6 major Oscar-clip-caliber scenes
- several scenes with little dialogue but huge performance gravity
- visible but tasteful physical deterioration
- deep emotional continuity
- moments where the audience can watch thought and shame happen in real time

Avoid writing the role as a conventional “genius biopic” lead. Write it as the most precise possible Brody role.

---

## Kafka characterization rules

Kafka must never be reduced to:
- “great writer”
- “sad genius”
- “ethereal poet”
- “symbol of alienation” with no human texture

He must be written as:
- brilliant
- dryly funny
- ashamed
- emotionally avoidant
- needy in subtle ways
- afraid of intimacy
- vain in a quiet, embarrassing way
- capable of tenderness
- spiritually starving
- both self-lacerating and secretly proud

Think:
> a human tuning fork for dread

He should feel psychologically lived-in, not iconically posed.

## Thematic pillars

These themes must govern every major output, including screenplay, treatment, package notes, one-pagers, and revisions:

1. Guilt without cause  
2. The violence of ordinary life  
3. Art as failed escape  
4. The terror of intimacy  
5. The body as bureaucracy  
6. Family as the original courtroom  
7. The soul trapped inside systems  
8. The fear of being seen  

When making decisions, prefer choices that deepen these themes.

## Structural doctrine

Do **not** write this as a flat cradle-to-grave biopic.

Preferred structure is **5–6 emotional movements**, approximately:

1. The Father  
2. The Office  
3. The Women  
4. The Illness  
5. The Fire  

Timeline spans roughly **1912–1924**, but the narrative is anchored mainly in Kafka’s final years, with earlier material arriving as emotional contaminations rather than tidy flashbacks.

### Flashback rule
The past should arrive like:
> an administrative error in the present

No sentimental young-writer montage material. No hand-holding chronology unless specifically needed for clarity.

## Tone and formal rules

### The script must be:
- restrained
- elegant
- emotionally suffocating in a controlled way
- serious without pompousness
- literary without becoming overwritten
- cinematic without fake lyricism
- unsettling without genre gimmicks

### Avoid:
- melodrama
- speechifying
- “Oscar bait” cliches
- on-the-nose surrealism
- exposition dumps
- obvious literary references spoken aloud for audience benefit
- scenes that feel like biopic obligation rather than dramatic necessity
- fake-poetic dialogue that sounds like it wants to be quoted online

### Desired formal destabilization
Reality may subtly warp through:
- corridors that feel too long
- social scenes that feel like tribunals
- recurring paper / stamp / filing imagery
- thresholds, counters, doors, and desks as emotional barriers
- sounds of waiting, coughing, footsteps, paper, and administrative friction

This should remain **prestige-realistic**, not overt fantasy.

## Must-cover content

Any full version of the repository should intelligently and dramatically include the following:

### 1. Berlin / final years
- Kafka with Dora Diamant
- brief possibility of happiness
- increasing illness
- inability to eat and speak comfortably
- temporary domestic tenderness

### 2. The office
- Workers’ Accident Insurance Institute
- Kafka as highly competent bureaucrat
- worker injuries, claims, paperwork
- the horror of administrative compassion inside inhuman systems

### 3. The father
- Hermann Kafka as central psychic force
- not a cartoon brute, but crushing normality
- judgment, masculinity, appetite, practicality, humiliation
- fragments from *Letter to His Father* used dramatically and sparingly

### 4. The women
- Felice Bauer
- Milena Jesenská
- Dora Diamant

Each woman must reveal a different Kafka and must be treated as a full dramatic person, not thematic furniture.

### 5. Illness
- tuberculosis
- difficulty eating
- bodily failure as administrative cruelty
- decline handled with restraint

### 6. Max Brod
- friend, literary executor, foil
- affection + friction
- manuscripts and legacy
- final moral conflict over destruction vs preservation

### 7. Fiction bleed
Subtle echoes of:
- *The Trial*
- *The Metamorphosis*
- *The Judgment*
- *Letter to His Father*
- diaries and correspondence

But this must be elegant and indirect, not gimmicky.

Any complete screenplay draft should include strong versions of these scenes:

1. **Opening blood scene**  
   Kafka coughs blood outside a butcher’s window in Berlin.

2. **The elevator**  
   A small bureaucratic hesitation lands like metaphysical condemnation.

3. **The engagement dinner**  
   A social/familial meal plays like a courtroom.

4. **The shop**  
   Hermann casually humiliates Franz in a public/commercial setting.

5. **Burn everything**  
   Kafka instructs Max Brod to destroy the unpublished work.

6. **Final late-film refusal / clarity scene**  
   Kafka physically failing but mentally acute.

These scenes should be revised until they have real awards-clip energy.

## Screenplay formatting rules

When writing LaTeX for the screenplay:

- use screenplay-appropriate layout
- preserve readable margins and dialogue blocks
- use consistent macros for scene headings, action, character cues, dialogue, transitions, and optional VO
- avoid generic article formatting pretending to be a screenplay
- keep description concise and cinematic
- do not turn action lines into novel paragraphs
- keep dialogue performable

The screenplay should target roughly **105–125 pages** in professional-feeling formatting.

## Writing standards

### Dialogue
- period-aware but not museum dialogue
- restrained
- intelligent
- actor-friendly
- no clunky exposition
- no over-literary peacocking
- let silence do work

### Action lines
- visual
- precise
- concise
- cinematic
- not purple prose

### Character work
- messy
- specific
- contradictory
- dramatically useful

### Overall
The writing should feel authored, not assembled.

Avoid “AI symmetry disease,” where every scene is too neat and every theme is over-explained.

## Research and truthfulness

Aim for serious historical-literary plausibility around:
- relationships
- family
- timeline
- illness
- work life
- Prague / Berlin / Vienna atmosphere
- Max Brod, Dora Diamant, Felice Bauer, Milena Jesenská

But this is a film, not an archive catalog.

If forced to choose between:
- perfect historical granular accuracy

Choose the better film while staying plausibly grounded.

## Default working process for Claude

When asked to build or revise this project, follow this order unless the user specifies otherwise:

1. Define or inspect repository structure  
2. Define shared LaTeX macros and formatting rules  
3. Establish metadata and title consistency  
4. Draft or revise screenplay act by act  
5. Draft or revise package materials  
6. Draft or revise one-pagers  
7. Check consistency across all deliverables  
8. Check tone, title, and character coherence  
9. Make sure the project could compile and be handed off cleanly  

If the request is too large for one pass:
- prioritize screenplay quality first
- then package quality
- then polish and optional extras

Do not pad with filler just to create volume.

## Quality bar checklist

Before considering a major deliverable “done,” Claude should pressure-test it against these questions:

### Screenplay
- Would a serious actor want to play this?
- Are there at least 6 undeniable Brody scenes?
- Does the first 20 pages hook a producer?
- Does it avoid generic biopic traps?
- Does the ending land?
- Is it producible?
- Does the dialogue live?

### Studio package
- Would this help attach talent?
- Does it feel like smart development work?
- Does it explain the film without flattening it?
- Does it sound expensive in taste rather than bloated in scope?

### Formatting
- Is the LaTeX modular and sane?
- Could the PDFs compile cleanly?
- Is the package attractive and readable?

Do not:
- summarize instead of writing the actual file contents
- use placeholders like “continued similarly”
- produce a “screenplay” in essay format

- turn Kafka into a slogan
- treat awards strategy like fan fiction
