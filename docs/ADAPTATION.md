# Bring a book, article, play, interview, or recording

You do not need to know how to write a screenplay first. Give the assistant the material you can use, explain what matters to you, and ask:

> Help me turn this into a screenplay. Read the whole source, recommend the best movie inside it, ask me about important changes, and take it through revision and finished PDFs.

In Claude Code use `/story-adapt`; in Codex use `$story-adapt`. You can also select the skill from the assistant's skill menu.

The assistant preserves your original, reads the agreed material, explains what the film could keep or change, and asks you to decide. It then writes, reviews and revises the screenplay. It checks the adaptation against the source and makes the studio documents you selected. You can reject a suggested change or return to an earlier decision.

Tell it whether you wrote the material, have permission, believe it is public domain, or are unsure. Reading a publicly available article does not automatically grant adaptation rights. The assistant records what is known and what needs resolving; it cannot certify permission or legal clearance. If you are unsure, keep the status unresolved and ask the assistant to help identify the permission questions or explore a genuinely original alternative. Keeping a file private does not establish permission.

A fictional novel and a memoir both need adaptation choices. A memoir also needs fact checking when you promise a true story. “Inspired by” does not erase the need to track the material used or explain invented events. A recording needs an accurate transcript with time references; missing pages and unclear speech stay visible.

The standard for a beginner is the same: complete writing, substantive revision, supporting characters with meaningful choices, accurate supporting documents, and inspected PDF pages. No automatic tool can guarantee the same artistic result for every story. The assistant must explain remaining weaknesses instead of hiding them behind a pass label.

[Read the worked historical adaptation](../examples/adaptation/README.md) and [compare its two truth promises](../examples/true-story/README.md). The demonstration is a compact short, not evidence of a completed new feature-length workflow. Its assumptions and review limits are explicit.
