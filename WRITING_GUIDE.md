# Your first Story Factory session

You do not need a finished plot. Start with whatever you have: a person, a situation, an image, a relationship, or a question.

## 1. Bring the spark

In your AI assistant, describe it in your own words. For example:

> A retired lighthouse keeper starts receiving letters from ships that disappeared years ago. I want it to feel mysterious, but the relationship with her estranged daughter matters more than the mystery.

This is an invented example, not a story you have to use. Details like tone, a relationship, or something you want to avoid are helpful. It is fine to be unsure.

## 2. Ask for a conversation first

Copy the starter conversation from the [README](README.md), or use the [longer starter](site/story-starter.txt). Ask the assistant to explore with you before it drafts. One good question at a time is usually more useful than a long form.

You can say: “Show me three genuinely different directions,” “Be more specific,” “That feels too familiar,” or “Explain why you think that would work.” The assistant should help you choose, not quietly decide everything for you.

## 3. Build something you can look at

Once a direction feels right, ask for a short story brief: who it follows, what they want, what makes it difficult, and what may happen at the end. Read it. Change what does not feel like your story.

Then build the people and the outline. Ask what each character wants for themselves and which choices make the next scene happen. The outline should show a story moving forward, not just a collection of moods.

## 4. Write in manageable pieces

Ask for one sequence or section at a time. Read it as if you were watching the film. Is something actually happening? Do the people sound different? Can you see and hear the scene?

Save what you like. Tell the assistant clearly what to change and what to protect. A feature screenplay is a substantial project; it will usually take multiple sessions and revisions.

## 5. Invite useful criticism

After the full draft, ask: “What are the five biggest problems, and which scenes show them? What should we protect? Fix structure and character choices before polishing dialogue.”

An assistant that only praises the draft is not doing enough. You are looking for specific problems, practical alternatives, and a stronger next version.

## 6. Prepare to share

When the screenplay feels ready, ask for a short synopsis and a one-page introduction for the person you want to show it to. A director, actor, and potential producer need different information. The full project has more presentation templates if you need them.

Check that every summary describes the draft you actually wrote. Keep possible cast and partners clearly labelled as proposals. Review facts, permissions, and any financial assumptions before using the material professionally.

## How to pick up tomorrow

At the end of a session, ask the assistant:

> Give me a short handoff with our story decisions, the latest outline, what changed, unresolved questions, and the next thing to work on.

Save that alongside your latest writing. Paste both into your next session if your assistant cannot remember the project. In the complete file-based project, the assistant can also use the built-in progress tracker.

## If you want organized files and PDFs

Open a copy of this project in a coding assistant that can work with files. Tell it:

> Help me set up a new Story Factory project. I am here to write, so explain choices in plain language and handle the technical steps for me. Read the project instructions and begin with story discovery. Ask before installing software or doing anything that publishes or sends my work.

A helper can follow [the setup guide](docs/SETUP.md). Formatting a PDF needs extra software; ordinary writing and discussion do not. Your AI service's account, costs, and privacy rules still apply.
