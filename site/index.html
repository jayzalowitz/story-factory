02 / A LITTLE STRUCTURE. A LOT OF POSSIBILITY.

A writing room,
one conversation at a time.

You don’t have to solve the whole story at once. Each step gives you something to build on, and permission to change your mind.

↳

The important bit?

We work on the story before polishing the sentences.

A beautiful line is even better when it belongs in a scene that matters.

  1. 01

    Find the heart of it.

    Talk through your idea. Explore a few different directions. Find who it follows, what they want, and why it matters.

    Discovery & choosing a direction
  2. 02

    Give it people. Give it shape.

    Create characters with their own wants, then build scenes where their choices change what happens next.

    Characters & story structure
  3. 03

    Write the actual story.

    Move from an outline to real scenes. Dialogue, silences, a door that stays closed. Work in pieces you can read and respond to.

    Drafting
  4. 04

    Make the hard, good changes.

    Ask for specific criticism. Fix the big problems before fussing over a line. Keep what makes the story yours.

    Review & revision
  5. 05

    Get it ready for another reader.

    Check the details, then create a synopsis and a pitch that make someone want to open the screenplay.

    Presentation materials, final checks & handoff

The assistant carries the quality checks for you: a full-draft review, meaningful revisions, continuity, aligned pitches, and inspection of the final PDF pages. You do not need to know the specialist commands. It saves decisions and progress in your project folder. For a true story, research comes before the outline and a final fact check comes after the draft. You can return to an earlier step without deleting your writing.

Starting with a book or another source?

Use /story-adapt: “Adapt my novella into a contained feature. Preserve the ending and restrained humor; ask me before combining characters.” The assistant reads the source, helps choose what to preserve, and translates it into scenes. It keeps track of omissions and inventions. Whether the material is factual and whether you have adaptation permission are separate questions.

TRY A COMPLETE PROJECT

/story-new “Create a separate project called The Last Ferry.”

/story-office-hours “Two sisters inherit a ferry route neither wants.”

/story-run “Guide me from this idea to a revised screenplay, synopsis, and producer pitch. Ask me to choose the important creative turns.”

In Codex, replace the opening slash with a dollar sign. The assistant will pause for decisions, identify unfinished work, and save progress. A command does not guarantee a saleable script.

Already have a script?

Put a copy in your project folder. Start with /story-studio: “Read my draft. Protect its dry tone. Give me the most valuable changes as choices before rewriting.” Choose accept, modify, defer, or decline. Your draft stays yours.

Working from real events?

Say “based on a true story” or “inspired by a true story” at the start. Use /story-research before drafting. The assistant separates facts, disputed claims, and invention; the evidence stays connected to the writing. Run /story-truth again on the finished draft. Research does not clear rights or replace legal advice.

06 / FROM “I DON’T HAVE AN ACCOUNT” TO YOUR FIRST SESSION

You don’t have to be
the technical person.

You need a computer, a folder for your story, and an AI assistant that can work with that folder. Choose one route. You do not need subscriptions to all of them.

  1. Choose your writing assistant.

    Claude Code: open the official desktop guide. Download Claude, create an account or sign in, and use its Code tab for a local project folder. “Code” describes the file-working tool; you can ask it to write a screenplay in ordinary English.

    Codex: follow the official app guide. Install the supported app, sign in with your ChatGPT account, choose Codex, and open your local folder. See current plan and usage details before paying.

    Each provider sets its own eligibility, pricing, and usage limits. Choose a plan that includes the file-working tool you intend to use; a basic chat subscription may differ. Use the provider’s sign-in flow. Story Factory needs no separate developer API key, and includes no model credits.

    Can I use Grok or an ordinary web chat?

    Yes, for a manual writing conversation. Create or sign into an account at Grok’s official site, or use your preferred chat service. Copy the starter prompt, add your idea, and paste it into a conversation. Save the writing yourself. Native Story Factory commands, folder automation, and parallel researchers are supported here through Claude Code and Codex; a Grok command integration has not been tested.

  2. Get your own copy of Story Factory.

    Download the toolkit ZIP ↓

    A ZIP is a folder packed into one download. Find it in Downloads, double-click it to unpack it, and move the resulting Story Factory folder somewhere you can find again, such as Documents. On Windows, use Extract All. You do not need a GitHub account to download the public toolkit.

  3. Open that folder in the assistant.

    Start a local session and use its folder chooser to select Story Factory. Ordinary browser chat cannot install commands into your computer’s folders. Paste the request below into the assistant’s message box. It will inspect the included installer and do the setup for you.

    If Python is missing, ask the assistant to walk you through installing it. Approve the request to read and save files in this chosen folder and run its included installer. The assistant should explain any system-tool installation separately. Writing can begin now; when you want PDFs it checks Tectonic (or XeLaTeX/latexmk) and Poppler, then helps with missing tools.

  4. Start a separate story folder.

    Close and reopen the session if the new commands do not appear. In Claude Code, type /story-new. In Codex, type $story-new or find it through /skills. Add: “Create a separate project named My Film in Documents. Keep my toolkit unchanged.”

    The assistant creates your working files and tells you where they are. Open that new story folder for the rest of your sessions. If a command is missing, say: “Check that this is the right folder and verify the Story Factory installation.”

  5. Write, choose, revise, and collect the work.

    Use Bring your idea to prepare your brief. Paste it into your story session. Begin with /story-office-hours, or ask /story-run to guide the complete process. For Codex, use $ in place of /.

    Read the assistant’s questions and choose the important story decisions. Ask /story-studio for development notes on an existing draft. Select only the package pieces you want. The assistant should review craft and representation, revise against scene evidence, and check real-world claims when relevant.

    When the writing is ready, ask /story-check to help prepare and inspect your selected PDFs. It may need free document-building tools; ask it to explain and set those up then. Finish with /story-handoff. Ask: “Show me the folder containing my editable files, the PDFs that actually passed checks, and any remaining concerns.” Keep a backup. Use /story-resume next time.

Your work stays under your control. The toolkit is public; a new story you write in your own folder is not automatically published. Your assistant provider processes what you share with it under its own terms. This website’s prompt builder sends nothing to an AI service.

08 / BEFORE YOU GO

You don’t need to have
it all figured out.

A few things that might make the first step easier.

Still just a feeling?

“I know what I want it to feel like, but I don’t have a plot.”

That counts. Bring it.

Start there →
Do I need to know how to code?

No. Start by copying a prompt into your AI assistant and having a conversation. The complete file-based project has extra tools for organizing the work and making PDFs. A coding assistant or a helper can handle that setup when you want it.

Does this website write the screenplay?

This website helps you prepare your idea and your opening prompt. The writing happens in the AI assistant you choose. Story Factory supplies the process and templates; you make the creative decisions.

What if I already have a draft?

Choose “A draft that needs another look” in the idea builder. Ask your assistant to read your work, identify the most important problems, and tell you what to protect before suggesting revisions.

Can I adapt a novel, memoir, article or play?

Yes. Choose the adaptation starting point or tell your assistant what you have. Keep your original file. The assistant will help read the selected material, agree what matters, turn it into cinematic scenes, and review changes against the source. A memoir may also need factual research. Source access alone does not establish adaptation permission.

Is this only for serious dramas?

No. A comedy, thriller, romance, or speculative film needs its own rhythm and rules. The templates are designed for feature films, but they do not prescribe a genre, an actor, or a particular mood.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

This introduction and prompt builder need no account or payment. Your chosen AI assistant may require an account and charge for use. Story Factory does not include AI service credits or run an AI model for you.

Who sees the idea I type here?

The builder works in your browser. It does not upload or save your notes, and this site has no analytics. Refreshing clears the form. Once you copy your idea into another service, that service’s privacy rules apply.

Will this make my story ready to sell?

It can help you develop a draft and presentation materials. There are no promises of representation, financing, casting, sales, or awards. Read and revise the work yourself, verify important facts, and check rights and permissions before professional use.

What about the complete project?

The complete toolkit is public on GitHub, including the detailed guide, both sets of skills, editable templates, and examples. Follow Get set up to use it on your computer. Your own story folder is separate from this public repository.
