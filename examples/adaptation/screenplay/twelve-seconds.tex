\vspace*{2in}\begin{center}TWELVE SECONDS\\[18pt]A historical short\\[18pt]Story Factory demonstration\end{center}
\vfill\small Based on the documented flights of December 17, 1903. All dialogue and private exchanges are invented. Adapted from three National Park Service historical accounts. This is a compact demonstration, not a feature or biography.\normalsize
\newpage\setcounter{page}{1}
FADE IN:\par
\scene{EXT. KILL DEVIL HILLS - MORNING}

Wind combs the sand back over two footprints as quickly as the boots can make them.\par

WILBUR WRIGHT holds the edge of a wing. ORVILLE kneels at its skid. Both are dressed rather better than their surroundings deserve.\par

Orville draws a screw from his coat pocket. Sand comes with it.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}That the last one?\par\end{quote}

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}The last loose one.\par\end{quote}

Wilbur moves his grip. The fabric trembles, a small captive animal.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Don't let go.\par\end{quote}

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}I had understood that part.\par\end{quote}

Orville seats the screw. It turns once, then refuses. He backs it out, clears the sand on his cuff and starts again.\par

A gust lifts the wing until Wilbur has to put his weight on it. Orville stops working. Watches his brother's shoes skid.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}We can wait.\par\end{quote}

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}We can.\par\end{quote}

Neither moves away. Orville finishes the screw.\par

% scene: S002
\scene{EXT. LAUNCH RAIL - LATER}

Volunteers from the lifesaving station take places around the machine. JOHN DANIELS stands at a camera already aimed down the rail.\par

Orville studies the narrow path ahead. The sand beyond it offers no reassurance.\par

Wilbur extends his hand. Orville shakes it.\par

The engine starts. Conversation disappears.\par

Orville settles into the cradle. Tests the controls once. Wilbur steadies the wing. They look at each other: the decision is no longer discussable.\par

Orville releases the restraint.\par

The machine travels down the rail. Wilbur runs beside it, hand raised to the wing. At first the distance between wood and sand is difficult to see.\par

Then there is distance.\par

Daniels presses the shutter.\par

Wilbur takes another stride with his hand still raised. The wing has left it behind.\par

The machine pitches. Orville moves, corrects too far, corrects again. The sand comes up. A short, ugly landing.\par

The engine stops.\par

Orville remains where he is. His hands still hold the controls.\par

Wilbur arrives, breathing hard. He touches the wing before he touches his brother.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}Anything broken?\par\end{quote}

Orville looks down at himself, then at the skid.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Which of us?\par\end{quote}

Wilbur helps him out.\par

% scene: S003
\scene{EXT. SAND BESIDE THE RAIL - MOMENTS LATER}

A measuring tape runs back toward the rail. Orville holds one end at the landing mark. Wilbur reads the distance.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}A hundred and twenty feet.\par\end{quote}

Orville looks from the mark to the rail. From here it seems a terribly short trip for so much preparation.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Time?\par\end{quote}

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}Twelve seconds.\par\end{quote}

A volunteer begins lifting the tape. Orville holds his end down a moment longer, preserving the line between the two places.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}All right.\par\end{quote}

He lets go. The tape disappears into its case.\par

Wilbur turns toward the machine. Orville walks with him.\par

% scene: S004
\scene{EXT. LAUNCH RAIL AND SAND - DAY}

The machine goes back to its beginning.\par

Wilbur takes the controls this time. Orville steadies the wing. The same preparations, done with less uncertainty and no less care.\par

A flight. A landing. Men carrying what cannot yet carry itself across sand.\par

Orville flies again. The distance lengthens. So does the walk back.\par

Before the fourth attempt, Wilbur checks the machine. Orville squats beside him, watching a fastening settle into place.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}You needn't watch every screw.\par\end{quote}

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Only the ones holding you up.\par\end{quote}

Wilbur gives it another quarter turn.\par

The fourth flight rises. Orville runs with it, then stops. He follows on foot as the flyer continues away.\par

It lasts long enough for waiting to become a different kind of work.\par

At the far end, Wilbur brings it down.\par

% scene: S005
\scene{EXT. FOURTH LANDING SITE - LATER}

The brothers stand beside the machine. They have measurements to write down and no suitable surface.\par

Orville braces a notebook against his thigh.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Eight hundred and fifty-two.\par\end{quote}

He writes.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}Fifty-nine seconds.\par\end{quote}

Orville writes that too. He checks the numbers against what they have just seen, as though one might invalidate the other.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}We should tell Father.\par\end{quote}

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}After lunch.\par\end{quote}

A gust takes the loose edge of the notebook. Orville clamps it shut.\par

The wing rises.\par

Wilbur grabs for it. Orville drops the notebook into his coat and catches a strut. The machine pulls both men sideways.\par

A shout from a volunteer. Hands reach for fabric and wood.\par

The flyer rolls. The brothers lose their grip.\par

A crack. Then several smaller sounds they know too well.\par

The machine lies still in the sand. The wind keeps working.\par

Wilbur approaches the damaged engine. Orville stays at the wing he could not hold.\par

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}Can we repair it?\par\end{quote}

Wilbur doesn't answer immediately. He examines the damage instead of offering comfort.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}Not here.\par\end{quote}

Orville nods. He frees a strip of fabric caught under his boot. Puts it down carefully, out of the wind.\par

% scene: S006
\scene{EXT. SAND BESIDE THE DAMAGED FLYER - AFTERNOON}

The machine has been secured against another gust. Nothing in it moves now.\par

Orville opens the notebook. A grain of sand sits in the middle of the figures. He brushes it away without smearing them.\par

Wilbur joins him. Looks at the page.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}That will sound small.\par\end{quote}

\character{ORVILLE}
\begin{quote}It was small.\par\end{quote}

Wilbur looks back at the distance they crossed.\par

Orville tears a blank scrap from the rear of the notebook. Starts a message. Stops before the first word and reads the measurements again.\par

\character{WILBUR}
\begin{quote}Tell him what happened.\par\end{quote}

Orville writes. Wilbur holds the scrap flat against the wind.\par

We cannot read the message. Only the two hands keeping the paper still.\par

Beyond them the damaged machine rests on the sand, and the empty air above it holds nothing at all.\par

\begin{flushright}FADE OUT.\end{flushright}

\begin{flushright}THE END\end{flushright}
