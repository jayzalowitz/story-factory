# Full-draft targeted review

Authoring-agent review of four complete scenes, approximately five screenplay pages plus cover. No independent reviewer, test audience or native Claude/Codex run is claimed.

The desired tone is warm and lightly dry. Inez wants a perfect last performance that would postpone acknowledging Jo's move. Jo wants to leave without being made responsible for the group's future. The conflict costs Inez the imagined flawless record and costs Jo the option of disguising departure as an indefinitely renewable arrangement. Neither woman is reduced to delivering encouragement: Jo declines the proposed weekly return, and Inez chooses her own voice over a substitute instrument and requests permission to share the imperfect result. The ending keeps their separation while permitting joint credit.

Substantive targeted repairs before final build: removed a wrist-catching action that muddied whose decision it was to keep recording; Inez now takes her own hand away. Added the initial record-button action so the failed performance and Jo's interruption can actually exist in playback. Added an explicit new recording action before the spoken credit, avoiding an impossible post-stop capture. Kept the music undescribed as a named existing song, rather than inventing permission to use one.

Bechdel: PASS. In S003 Inez asks who will start the Thursday singing; Jo answers that Inez will. In S004 they discuss sharing the duet and using both names. Jo and Inez are both explicitly women; the actual exchanges concern their music and authorship, with no man as their topic. These conversations cause the story's decisions; they were not appended as test filler.

Continuity: both recorders are distinguished (audio device and wind instrument); the recording starts before the unsuccessful take, remains active through the interruption, is played back, then a new successful take and separate credit are recorded. The scarf moves from the case to Jo's hands and back. Both women carry the case out. The imperfect note survives by choice.

Remaining limits: this is a compact intimate chamber piece, with a deliberately modest reversal and one location. The musical performance itself has not been composed, produced or tested. The finished screenplay is the selected deliverable; no claim of a finished film or commercial readiness is made. Five dramatic pages are not a feature benchmark.
