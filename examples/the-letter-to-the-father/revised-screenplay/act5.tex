\movement{V. THE FIRE}

% ============================================================
% DORA — MÜRITZ
% ============================================================

\titlecard{MÜRITZ, BALTIC COAST, JULY 1923}

\scene{EXT. SUMMER CAMP -- MÜRITZ -- DAY}

\action{A holiday camp for Eastern European Jewish children. Simple wooden buildings near the sea. The sound of children playing. Yiddish, Hebrew, German mingling in the summer air.}

\action{Kafka, 40 now and visibly fragile, sits on a bench in the shade. He is watching the children with an expression of such gentle attention that it borders on pain.}

\action{DORA DIAMANT, 25, comes out of the camp kitchen carrying a tray of fish and black bread. Dark-haired. Direct. Built for work, not display.}

\action{She sees the thin, dark-eyed man on the bench turning a fish over in his fingers, checking again for bones while one of the smaller boys already reaches for it.}

\character{DORA}
\paren{approaching}
\dialogue{Don't give him that. He'll swallow a bone and they'll blame the kitchen.}

\action{Kafka looks up.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Then you've arrived in time to save us both.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Him certainly. You, we'll see.}

\action{She takes the fish from his hand, strips the bones out with quick, practiced fingers, and gives it to the boy.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Eat slowly. Once in your life.}

\action{The boy runs off. Dora looks at Kafka's empty hands.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{You're from the kitchen?}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Dora Diamant.\\
From Będzin, Poland.\\
Kitchen, laundry, occasional rescue work.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Franz Kafka. Prague.\\
My doctor prescribed sea air and obedience.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{And have you disappointed him yet?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Continuously.}

\action{She laughs. Not politely, but with recognition. Kafka watches her a beat too long.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% DORA AND FRANZ — WALKING
% ============================================================

\scene{EXT. BEACH -- MÜRITZ -- EVENING}

\action{They walk along the shoreline. The light is gold but the water is cold-looking. Dora carries her shoes in one hand.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{At home my father had the whole thing arranged.\\
Husband by nineteen. Children. Respectability.\\
Proper worries on schedule.}

\action{Kafka smiles. A real smile. The kind of smile that changes his entire face.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{I wanted Hebrew. Work. Palestine, maybe. At minimum a key that answered only to me.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{In Prague even the key tends to belong to my father.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Then stop calling it your key.}

\action{That lands more cleanly than anything sympathetic would have. They keep walking.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{The camp secretary says you write. The children say you murder fish. I believe the children.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'm a retired insurance inspector who writes. There's a difference.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{What do you write about?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Impossible situations. A man wakes up an insect. Another is put on trial and never told why. Animals give lectures.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Then you've been preparing for ordinary life.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{surprised}
\dialogue{Most people flinch.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Most people prefer lies with good table manners.}

\action{Kafka looks at her and loses the careful little speech he had ready. Dora keeps walking, trusting him to catch up. He does.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE CHOICE
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. DOCTOR'S OFFICE -- PRAGUE -- DAY}

\action{A consulting room. Not Mühlstein's --- a newer doctor, younger, with a chart that has too many entries. DR. KODYM reviews the latest X-ray without enthusiasm.}

\character{DR. KODYM}
\dialogue{Berlin is inadvisable. The damp. The cold. The lack of consistent medical supervision.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I understand.}

\character{DR. KODYM}
\dialogue{I want to be clear. A Prague sanatorium, even your sister's farm, gives you time. Berlin in winter, without proper care, takes it away.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{How much time?}

\action{The doctor pauses. Kafka waits with the patience of a man who has spent his professional life reading evasions in claim forms.}

\character{DR. KODYM}
\dialogue{I'm not in a position to---}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Estimate.}

\action{Kodym closes the chart.}

\character{DR. KODYM}
\dialogue{Stay in Prague, under care: a year, perhaps more. Berlin, as you describe it: considerably less.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Thank you, Doctor. That was a clear report.}

\action{He stands. Takes his hat. Kodym watches him go with the expression of a man who has just been understood perfectly and obeyed not at all.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% BERLIN — THE APARTMENT
% ============================================================

\titlecard{BERLIN, SEPTEMBER 1923}

\scene{EXT. BERLIN STREET -- DAY}

\action{Kafka and Dora walk toward Steglitz carrying what they own between them. They pass the Askanischer Hof hotel. Kafka's step catches. Half a second. Dora notices. Neither speaks.}

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- DAY}

\action{A small, cold apartment. Top floor. Cheap furniture. Damp walls.}

\action{Kafka and Dora are moving in together. She arranges plates in a kitchen so small that two people cannot stand in it at the same time. He carries a box of books --- his only real possessions --- and sets it down.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{The stove works. I tested it this morning.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Does the landlord know about me?}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{I told her my husband was arriving.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{a flicker}
\dialogue{Your husband.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{My husband-to-be, then. My--- person.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{My person. I've been called worse.}

\action{Dora takes a small brass key from her coat pocket and drops it into his palm.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{One for you. It sticks. Lift, then turn.}

\action{She puts down the plates. Turns to him. Takes his face in her hands. His face, which is thinner now, the bones more visible, the dark circles deeper.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Franz. This is ours. Cold, tiny, awful. Ours. No father in the next room. No doctor at the door. Just us.}

\action{He puts his hands over hers. His hands are cold. Hers are warm.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{The doctor in Prague said I shouldn't come.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{I know. Ottla wrote to me.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{And you let me come anyway.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{I don't let you do anything. You chose this.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{quietly}
\dialogue{I know what it costs.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Then don't waste it.}

\action{He closes his hand around the key. Says ``just us'' once, then under his breath again, testing it.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% BERLIN — THE ROOM MEETS THE STATE
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. DISTRICT REGISTRATION OFFICE -- BERLIN -- DAY}

\action{A municipal room of benches, inkpads, impatience. Berliners wait with folders, ration slips, babies, coughs. A CLERK at a high counter works through the queue with ink on his cuff and half a cheese roll going stale beside the stamp pad.}

\action{Dora and Kafka reach the counter. Dora has the lease. Kafka keeps a handkerchief at his mouth and wills himself not to use it.}

\character{CLERK}
\dialogue{Name.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Diamant. And Franz Kafka. He lives with me.}

\character{CLERK}
\dialogue{Relation?}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Future husband.}

\character{CLERK}
\dialogue{No such entry. Employment?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Former legal secretary to the Workers' Accident Insurance Institute in Prague.}

\character{CLERK}
\dialogue{Current income?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Under paragraph twelve, co-residence may be recorded pending papers if the tenant accepts liability.}

\action{The clerk looks up for the first time. Studies him: the dark suit, the careful diction, the handkerchief he is pretending not to need.}

\character{CLERK}
\dialogue{Thirty days. Then bring papers or I remove him.}

\action{STAMP. Across the bottom, in purple ink: `LODGER. TEMPORARY.'}

\scene{EXT. DISTRICT REGISTRATION OFFICE -- CONTINUOUS}

\action{They step back into the Berlin street. Tram noise. November wind. Dora runs her thumb once over the purple word `TEMPORARY,' folds the paper back on its old crease, and hands it to Kafka.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{`Lodger. Temporary.' Charming.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Better than omitted.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Only slightly.}

\action{She folds the paper, puts it in her coat, threads her arm through his, and walks him home.}

% ============================================================
% DOMESTIC LIFE — BRIEF HAPPINESS
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- EVENING}

\action{A small domestic scene. Dora cooks --- something simple. Kafka sits at the table, reading to her from a Hebrew textbook. They are studying together. His pronunciation is careful, bookish. Hers is fluid, natural.}

\character{DORA}
\paren{correcting him}
\dialogue{No, no. The accent is on the second syllable. You sound like a German professor.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I am a German professor.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{You're a Czech insurance clerk who thinks he's a German professor. It's entirely different.}

\action{He laughs. Dora turns from the stove to look at him.}

\action{She brings the food to the table. Soup. Bread. A small portion. Kafka looks at it.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Eat.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'm not---}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Eat. The soup is for eating.}

\action{She tears the bread into smaller pieces, skims a little fat from the top of the soup with the edge of the spoon, and nudges the bowl closer to him. He eats. Slowly. Carefully. Dora keeps talking while she watches the spoon travel back and forth.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% DOMESTIC — BERLIN MORNINGS
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- MORNING}

\action{The apartment is tiny. Kafka writes at the table. Dora moves around him in the kitchen. He lifts an elbow; she opens a cupboard. He lowers it; she passes behind him with the kettle.}

\action{Beside his notebook: an opened letter on Institute stationery. The Kraus case resolved --- full compensation, guards in all three mills. He has folded Marschner's note back into exact thirds.}

\action{She puts her hand on his head as she passes. A gesture so casual it might be the most intimate thing anyone has ever done to him.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Nobody's father is in the next room. No doctor is scheduling my meals.\\
I can hear myself think. That's new enough.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{And what do you think?}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{surprised by his own answer}
\dialogue{That I'm happy.}

\action{He hears himself say it. Looks down. Dora smiles and says nothing.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE LETTERS TO VIENNA
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- EVENING}

\action{Dora comes in from errands. Kafka is at the table, writing. Not a story --- a letter. Several pages already finished. The handwriting is rapid and small.}

\action{She sets down a bag of groceries. Sees the pages. Sees the salutation: ``Dear Milena.''}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{How many pages?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Seven. She asked about the aphorisms. It's a literary---}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Seven pages.}

\action{She begins putting food away. Plates. A turnip. Bread. Her movements are harder than usual.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Dora, it's not---}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Don't tell me what it isn't. Tell me why you can write seven pages to a woman in Vienna about what you feel, and you live with me and say `I'm happy.' Two words. I get two words and she gets an essay.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{That isn't fair.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{No. Fair is not the word for this.}

\action{She sits across from him. Direct. Not tearful --- angry in the way of a woman who has staked her life on someone and wants the full weight of him, not the version he sends by post.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{You write to her about fear. About your father. About what the illness means. I sleep beside you every night and you say the soup is good and the Hebrew lesson went well.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I don't want to bring that into this room.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{It's already in this room. It sleeps here. It coughs here. It sits at this table.}

\action{Kafka looks at the letter. At Dora. At the distance between the table and the woman three feet away.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Which one is the letter and which one is the life? Because you have done this before, Franz. You know you have. And they left.}

\action{That lands. A long silence. The turnip and the bread sit on the counter, not put away.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{very quietly}
\dialogue{I'm afraid that if I say it out loud here, it becomes real. And then this stops being the place where I'm safe.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{It is not safe. I am not safe. I am here. That is a different thing.}

\action{He reaches for her hand. She lets him take it. But she does not soften.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Finish the letter if you need to. But don't tell me you're happy and then tell Vienna the truth.}

\action{Kafka folds the letter to Milena. Places it in the drawer beside the thick, still-tied packet from Prague. Two documents addressed to people not in the room.}

\action{He looks at Dora.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Ask me again.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{What do you think?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{That I'm terrified. That the illness is ahead of me. That I hear my father's voice every time I try to eat. That I chose Berlin because I wanted one true thing before the end, and you are the true thing, and I am ruining it by being exactly who I've always been.}

\action{Dora's face does not break. Her hand tightens on his.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Better.}

\action{She gets up. Finishes putting away the groceries. He watches her. After a while, he pulls a fresh page toward him. This time he does not write to Vienna.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE ROOM BETWEEN
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- NIGHT}

\action{Kafka is alone. Dora is out. The apartment is four steps from table to stove.}

\action{He rises from the table. Walks to the stove. Four steps. But the four steps take longer than they should. The room does not stretch --- the distances stay the same. It is the time that stretches. Each step lands in its own silence.}

\action{He reaches the stove. Touches it. Cold. The room is the room again. Four steps.}

\action{He walks back to the table. Sits. The apartment is tiny and ordinary and his.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE LETTER RETURNS
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- AFTERNOON}

\action{Rain at the window. Dora sorts the post. One envelope. One small brown-paper parcel with a Prague postmark. Julie Kafka's hand on the label.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{From your mother.}

\action{Kafka takes the parcel. The paper is worn at the folds, the corners rounded and dark from handling.}

\action{He opens it carefully. Inside: a thick manuscript bundle in his own handwriting. The Letter. A smaller note in Julie's hand. And loose at the bottom of the parcel, a postcard in Hermann's broad handwriting, addressed to Franz at the Berlin address and clearly dropped in by Julie as an afterthought.}

\action{INSERT --- Julie's note: ``Franz --- I could not give it to him. I kept it too long already. Forgive me. Mother.''}

\action{INSERT --- Hermann's postcard: ``The shop is busy. The new girl cannot count change. Your mother sends regards. Eat properly. Father.''}

\action{Kafka reads the note. Then the postcard. Then holds them both --- one hundred pages that never reached his father, and five lines that did.}

\action{Dora watches him.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{What is it?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{A hundred pages to my father. And a postcard from him. They have never been in the same room before.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{And now?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Now it has come back to me. Still unopened.}

\action{He runs his thumb along the edge of the pages --- not opening them, just feeling their thickness.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Will you read it?}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{after a beat}
\dialogue{No. I remember it well enough.}

\action{He places the packet in the table drawer. Closes it. The drawer does not shut cleanly. Too much paper inside.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE BURNING
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- NIGHT}

\action{Late. The apartment is cold. The stove provides the only heat and the only light, its door open, the flames visible.}

\action{Kafka sits on the floor near the stove. Before him: a stack of manuscript pages. Handwritten. Notebooks. Loose pages. The accumulated work of years.}

\action{Dora sits behind him, legs folded, watching.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I want to burn these.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Are you sure?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{They're unfinished. If I leave them, someone will mistake that for permission.}

\action{He picks up a notebook. Opens it. Reads a line. His face, in the firelight, is calm.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{This one is about a woman who turns into a bridge. I never found the ending.}

\action{He places it in the stove. The pages curl. The fire accepts them.}

\action{He picks up another. And another. Stories, fragments, beginnings without endings. Each one goes into the fire with a strange, ceremonial tenderness.}

\action{Dora watches. She does not protest. Each notebook waits its turn on her knee before passing into his hand.}

\action{At the edge of the stack, separate from the notebooks, lies the thick packet returned from Prague. Dora sees it.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Not that one?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{No.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Why not?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Because it isn't unfinished. It's undeliverable. Different problem.}

\action{The last notebook. He holds it longer than the others.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{What is that one?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I don't remember. That's probably the best reason to burn it.}

\action{He puts it in the fire. Watches it burn.}

\action{The apartment is warm now. He leans back against Dora. She wraps both arms around him. They sit without speaking and watch the pages cave inward to ash.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% BURN EVERYTHING — OSCAR CLIP SCENE
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- DAY}

\action{A different day. Kafka is at the table, sorting manuscript pages into exact piles. His hand is steady, but slower than it used to be.}

\action{At one end of the table, separate from the manuscript piles, sits the thick packet returned from Prague. Still tied.}

\action{Brod has come to visit. He sits across from him, coat still on, hat still in both hands. He has traveled from Prague. Kafka is thinner than the last time they met.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{You look---}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Don't make me comfort you.}

\action{Brod stops. Nods once.}

\action{His eyes go to the packet at the end of the table.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{And that?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{That's different.\\
My mother wouldn't take it into his room.}

\action{Brod takes that in.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'm not giving you that.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{Because it's private?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Because it was addressed. That's the difference.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{There is something practical. About the manuscripts.}

\action{Brod goes still.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Everything in my desk in Prague. The novels. The notebooks. The stories. Everything unpublished. Burn it.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{Unread?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Especially unread.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{Franz, if you wanted them burned, you would have burned them yourself.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I burned what was here. The rest are in Prague. In your reach.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{No. In your trust. That's different.}

\action{Kafka says nothing. Brod presses.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{You're leaving me the crime and asking me to call it friendship.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'm leaving you instructions.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{For ``The Trial''? ``The Castle''?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Unfinished. Unpublishable.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{Alive.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Half-built isn't shelter, Max.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{Then let the rest of us stand in it.}

\action{The line lands. Kafka looks at him sharply.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{You think unfinished means failure. I think it means the door is still open.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{An open door is not the same thing as a house.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{No. But men have lived by less.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{You called writing prayer once.\\
I'm not feeding those pages to a stove.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Prayer is not public.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{No. Prayer is said to be heard.}

\action{Silence. Outside, Berlin moves through another day of its slow collapse.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{You always liked posterity better than obedience.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{And you would always rather burn it than leave it unfinished.}

\action{They hold each other's gaze. Neither persuades the other.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Max. As my friend. As the person I trust most in this world. Burn everything. Unread.}

\action{Brod looks at him for a long time.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{I'll take care of it.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{softer now}
\dialogue{That is not the same sentence.}

\character{BROD}
\dialogue{It's the sentence I can live with.}

\action{A beat. Then Kafka reaches across the table and takes Brod's hand. Brod covers it with his other hand. Neither lets go first.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{Thank you, Max.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% DORA'S FATHER — THE LETTER
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- DAY}

\action{Kafka writes a letter. Formal, careful. His best handwriting.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{You're really writing to him?}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{One should ask properly. Even if the answer is no.}

\action{Beside the paper: the thick parcel from Prague, unopened.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{The answer will be no. He's a Gerer Hasid. You're a secular Jew from Prague who writes stories about men turning into beetles.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'll leave out the beetles.}

\action{She sits beside him. Reads over his shoulder.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{``I am a man of modest means but sincere devotion.'' Franz, you're dying.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{without looking up}
\dialogue{Then at least the letter is honest.}

\action{He finishes the letter. Seals it. Addresses it to Dora's father in Będzin.}

\titlecard{THREE WEEKS LATER}

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- DAY}

\action{Dora holds a letter. She has already read it. Her face is composed but her hands are not.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{My father consulted the Gerer Rebbe.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{And?}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{The Rebbe advised against it.}

\action{A pause. Kafka nods once and squares Dora's father's letter with the edge of the table.}

\action{On the table beside Dora's letter: the thick parcel from Prague.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{I'm sorry.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{I'm not. I'm here. What does a rabbi in Poland know about us?}

\action{She sits beside him. Takes his hand. Through the window: a tram bell, a woman arguing over prices, a burst of laughter that turns into coughing. She keeps his hand in both of hers.}

\cutto

% ============================================================
% THE COLLAPSE
% ============================================================

\scene{INT. APARTMENT -- BERLIN-STEGLITZ -- NIGHT}

\action{Kafka in bed. Coughing. The cough is worse now --- deep, rattling, producing things he hides in his handkerchief before Dora can see.}

\action{Dora lies beside him. She is awake. She has been awake every night this week, listening to him breathe, counting the seconds between coughs.}

\character{DORA}
\paren{very quietly}
\dialogue{We need to go back. To Prague. To a doctor.}

\character{KAFKA}
\dialogue{If I go back to Prague, I go back to my father's apartment. I go back to the room I've been trying to leave my entire life.}

\character{DORA}
\dialogue{Franz. You need a hospital.}

\action{He doesn't answer. Another cough. This one longer, worse. When it ends, he lies very still, one hand at his ribs, waiting before he risks the next breath.}

\character{KAFKA}
\paren{barely above a whisper}
\dialogue{Not yet. Not yet.}

\action{She holds him. In the dark, in the cold, in Berlin, she holds him.}
