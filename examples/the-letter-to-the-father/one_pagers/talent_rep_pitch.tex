\textbf{Why This Is a Strong Agency / Management Package}\\
This is not a one-role project with decorative support. The Brody lead is the obvious center of gravity, but the surrounding cast is unusually attachable: Hermann, Brod, Felice, Milena, Dora, Julie, and Ottla all have clear dramatic jobs, real scenes, and clean actor-facing logic. That makes the project useful not only for one major star but for multiple clients across lead, supporting, and prestige-character lanes.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{The Anchor}\\
Adrien Brody as Kafka remains the package's defining proposition: a role with awards weight, physical transformation, silence-based acting, and six major clip scenes. That anchor matters because it gives reps a serious movie to take to other clients, not an unformed prestige aspiration waiting for a lead to explain itself.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{The Supporting Roles Are Real}\\
\textit{Hermann Kafka}: major supporting actor role, enormous authority, warmth and violence in the same instrument. --- \textit{Max Brod}: the friend, foil, and philosophical rival; not comic relief, but a second center of intelligence. --- \textit{Felice}: adult competence, clarity, and dignity, not passive fiancée material. --- \textit{Milena}: erotic and intellectual voltage, the shock of being read accurately. --- \textit{Dora}: warmth, presence, and the final movement's emotional answer to the whole film. --- \textit{Julie and Ottla}: family roles with genuine agency, not background function.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{Why Reps Can Place It Cleanly}\\
The movie is legible in one sentence, but richer on contact. Kafka, Brody, the father-letter, the office machine, the festival lane, the awards lane, the European prestige lane: all of it is easy to explain in a room. That matters for agencies and managers because the project does not require five calls just to clarify what kind of movie it is. It already knows what it is.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{Who This Serves}\\
Lead-actor reps looking for a career-phase role. Supporting-actor reps looking for one meaningful prestige part rather than generic festival wallpaper. Managers looking for a serious director collaboration vehicle. Talent teams packaging multiple clients into the same awards-quality project. The film is structured to reward that kind of strategic attachment rather than treating everyone outside the lead as background ornament.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{Why It Is Attractive Right Now}\\
Post-\textit{The Brutalist}, Brody gives the project immediacy. The market for severe adult prestige remains selective but real. And the film offers something many ``important'' scripts do not: it understands the difference between cultural prestige and actor value. Every meaningful role here comes with a precise dramatic function, a clean identity, and at least one scene an actor or rep can point to immediately.

\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{Packaging Use Case}\\
This is the kind of project an agency or management company can take out in layers: Brody as the anchor, a prestige director as the formal guarantor, then one or two major supporting pieces as the package firms up. It supports smart, staged attachment rather than requiring a miracle all at once.
