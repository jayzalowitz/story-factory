\section{Director Targets}

The shortlist prioritizes filmmakers who can do three things at once: preserve prestige realism, make institutions feel cinematic rather than inert, and protect the late Berlin warmth so the film does not collapse into one-note suffering.

\target{Brady Corbet}{Following \textit{The Brutalist}, Corbet has demonstrated the ambition, formal control, and comfort with extended runtime and psychological complexity that this material demands. He already has a working relationship with Brody and an understanding of prestige biographical drama at the highest level. The synergy is immediate.}{Dream / Strong Fit}

\target{Paweł Pawlikowski}{His work on \textit{Ida} and \textit{Cold War} demonstrates mastery of Central European period atmosphere, formal precision (aspect ratio, black-and-white, compressed framing), and the capacity to tell devastating emotional stories with extreme economy. Kafka's Prague and Pawlikowski's visual language are a natural match.}{Dream}

\target{Steve McQueen}{His range --- from the institutional horror of \textit{12 Years a Slave} to the intimate devastation of \textit{Shame} --- maps directly onto the terrain of \textit{The Letter to the Father}. McQueen's interest in the body under pressure and the individual inside systems makes this a thematically precise fit.}{Dream}

\target{Florian Henckel von Donnersmarck}{Director of \textit{The Lives of Others}, the definitive film about surveillance, institutional pressure, and the soul trapped inside systems. His German-language background and demonstrated comfort with Central European period production make him a natural choice.}{Strong Fit}

\target{László Nemes}{Following \textit{Son of Saul} and \textit{Sunset}, Nemes has established himself as the preeminent director of subjective, formally radical Central European historical drama. His close-frame, sensory approach could bring unprecedented intimacy to Kafka's perspective.}{Strong Fit}

\target{Joanna Hogg}{Her work on the \textit{Souvenir} films demonstrates an extraordinary sensitivity to the texture of memory, class dynamics, and emotional avoidance. The English-language shift would be significant, but her formal precision and psychological acuity are a strong match.}{Stretch}

\target{Cristian Mungiu}{Romanian New Wave rigor applied to Kafka's world. Mungiu's long-take, system-as-trap methodology (\textit{4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days}; \textit{Beyond the Hills}) is formally aligned with this material. His comfort with institutional settings and moral complexity is precise.}{Strong Fit}

\target{Edward Berger}{Following \textit{All Quiet on the Western Front} and \textit{Conclave}, Berger has demonstrated the capacity for both period spectacle and intimate institutional drama. His European production expertise and awards-season credibility make him a practical and artistically credible choice.}{Realistic}

\target{Jonathan Glazer}{The formal audacity of \textit{The Zone of Interest} --- telling a Holocaust story through absence and implication --- suggests a director capable of the indirect, formally controlled approach this material requires. The tonal match is exceptional.}{Dream}

\target{Céline Sciamma}{Her work on \textit{Portrait of a Lady on Fire} demonstrates the capacity for slow-burn emotional intensity within a period setting, with particular sensitivity to desire and restraint. A potentially revelatory choice.}{Stretch}

\target{Alice Rohrwacher}{Italian, formally inventive, comfortable with magical realism that never tips into fantasy. Her \textit{Happy as Lazzaro} shares Kafka's interest in the intersection of the mundane and the uncanny.}{Stretch}

\target{Lukas Dhont}{Following \textit{Close}, Dhont has shown extraordinary sensitivity to male vulnerability, emotional suppression, and the violence of social expectation. Younger director, but the sensibility is precise.}{Stretch}

\target{Agnieszka Holland}{Veteran Polish director with deep expertise in Central European historical drama (\textit{Europa Europa}, \textit{In Darkness}, \textit{Mr. Jones}). Extensive experience with the specific production landscape this film requires.}{Realistic}
