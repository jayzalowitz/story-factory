\section{Treatment}
\subsection{The ordinary ending of a job}
Clara Venn puts a saucer under a cabinet leg. In the Bellwether records depot, keeping a surface level is a professional skill. Everything is being classified for transfer or disposal. Tom Kest, the depot worker, has already set the boundary of his final shift. Clara checks his last ordinary crate and leaves the completion signature blank.

Jory Senn arrives in a van that barely clears the gate. Clara knows the turn; Jory has the handling gloves. The two women recognize competence before they like each other. A clean completion matters to both. Clara's retirement bridge payment depends on it. Jory's temporary employer has made permanent hours conditional on the same transfer. She is trying to secure a room near her daughter Nell's technical college.

Transition director Lida Rusk sets a physical deadline: Thursday nine. The contractor takes the building for conversion; the town cannot fund another week. A flood-reserve audit is approaching and every outstanding contract is being closed. The work is not glamorous enough to look political.

A thin blue ledger slips behind a carton. Jory catches it. Clara notices a pasted column: twenty-three housing entries marked SETTLED. A surviving slip marks household seventeen HELD. The page bears Clara's receiving signature. She stops the book going into a scanned-dispose carton.

Bea Quill arrives for stair rods removed when Low Steps was demolished twelve years earlier. The rods are a demand small enough to keep making after the larger demand has failed. She says she signed for a moving van, not compensation. Clara asks for the receipt without telling her what the book may mean. That night a carbon shows six held balances. Clara labels the series disputed, interrupting the clean completion Jory stayed late to help secure.

\subsection{Every solution belongs to somebody}
On Wednesday Clara asks registrar Mina Olt about preservation. Complaint, custody and adjudication are different acts. A disputed transfer needs an identified series, an owner release, an accountable officer and a legitimate receiving place. Clara initially avoids the municipal strongroom because her officer statement would expose her own act. She seeks outside custody instead. She does not get a free order to hold the building open. The problem cannot be solved simply by keeping one book in her handbag.

The red transfer status has already reached Jory's employer. Clara admits she cannot yet establish a debt. Jory wants a destination for the book so she can finish the rest. At Bea's flat the moving receipt is itemized on the reverse, including an overcharged tea break. Bea permits copies of her own documents, keeps other residents' papers in the tin, and insists that her home address stay out of public material. She has sewing work to complete; the town's belated interest does not own her day.

Lida suggests that the households may have elected moving assistance instead of title compensation. Clara searches for election forms. The dispatch cover says twenty-three attached, but no attachments exist. CONTENTS VERIFIED carries Clara's tick. Tom warns her against inventing a convenient memory.

The inquiry makes Clara miss a flat viewing Mara arranged. Clara tries to hand the cancellation to her daughter. Mara refuses. Her own move to a kitchen management job is going ahead Friday. Clara loses the holding fee and has to make the call herself. At the bridge layby, Jory learns from Bea that a household number can conceal multiple residents and different claims. A list of lawful tenants could erase the unrecognized residents again.

Jory asks Clara to use ordinary exception storage and sign clean completion. Clara instead crosses out the assurance that no outstanding rights depend on the disposed records. Jory sends the amended sheet to dispatch and waits for an answer that does not come.

\subsection{The record of their own acts}
Lida shows a separately authorized transfer of the relocation balance to the flood reserve. She made that decision. She argues that a threatened floodwall was a real public need, but she does not claim to have authorized a false receipt. The pasted column and the finance authority are not the same object.

During Tom's final meal break an old receiving-day tally emerges. Closing the original grant had required a deadline too. Clara was commended for getting it done. Jory tries to force an answer from operations, supplying a photograph of the household ledger. It moves beyond the contractor chain. Residents fear they may be asked to repay money. Ina Werr, an unrecognized rent payer, confronts both women at Bea's flat. She retrieves her slips, withholds contact authority and makes Bea strike the useful account of two rent payers. Bea must ask for consent again under altered conditions.

Clara confronts Jory, then accepts that her own red status changed Jory's work without consultation. The wrongs do not cancel. Jory starts a deletion and forwarding log, unable to guarantee that every copy is gone. Lida suspends unobserved access because private records have circulated. Tom's refusal to extend his last shift reduces their working window to ten.

A scale establishes that the original envelope could not contain twenty-three forms, but that alone proves little. Clara finds her own request for missing attachments and her own decision to close it. She copies both sides. At the counter, she admits the certification; Jory admits the disclosure. Bea supports preserving evidence, not a collective claim made in her name. Clara changes the proposed report from AFFECTED RESIDENTS REQUEST to I REPORT.

Preservation now means nine linked cartons, including adverse context and restricted welfare files. Courier storage fails because the lease and route package cannot guarantee six weeks. The municipal strongroom remains possible only through an itemized receiving process and Lida's release. Seven cartons are sealed before Tom leaves. Clara gives back her keys rather than make his final shift an indefinite emergency.

Nell proposes a commute instead of treating her education as her mother's private rescue project. Mara gives Clara dinner but refuses moral absolution. Late at the service office, Lida requires the finance instruction to travel with the complaint. Clara accepts that her own statement will hold up the bridge payment. Jory faces the employer's demand for clean completion. Their accurate packet becomes less useful as a simple accusation and more useful as evidence.

\subsection{Completion with exception}
Before dawn the two women lift the cartons onto dry pallets. Jory asks what happens if the review finds the households were paid. Clara admits she may have lost money merely to correct her own record, and that Jory's job cost may remain. There is no promised redemptive result.

Bea brings contact conditions: some consent, one silence, one wish to destroy the book. At06:30 Lida admits them for narrow access. Closed medical material is inventoried by folder. Bea contributes an unofficial eyewitness note without pretending it establishes entitlement. The finance total includes interest; SETTLED may refer to an account rather than people. Bea insists this inconvenient qualification stay in the packet.

Ina's refusal travels in a separate closed envelope, with no copy for Bea. Bea contributes only a note about her own key-return date. At the old counter she tells Clara that she originally asked about compensation, not rods. Clara apologizes without a speech. The women finish copies. Sana arrives to help handle the load and witness, not to endorse a claim. A flawed seal is voided and replaced. Jory photographs closed cartons instead of household pages.

Clara clears the building and surrenders access. At the receiving office a condition sheet must be joined to the declared inventory; all witnesses are present because they chose to come. Mina separates Clara's officer statement from the household privacy bundle. Clara leaves her own name on the public account of her certification. The nine cartons enter the strongroom at08:53. Mina retains the keys; Clara receives supervised review access, not ownership of interpretation.

Jory can still select NORMAL RETURN and obtain the employment letter without preventing custody. It would falsely close her own disclosure incident. She instead submits COMPLETE WITH EXCEPTION before nine. Lida confirms physical clearance. The building closes successfully while Jory's employment condition fails. Equipment is returned, the room cannot be secured, and a grievance opens with no promise of success. Mara leaves. Clara's payment is held and she seeks housing advice. Nell commutes while Jory works casual shifts.

Six weeks later the reserve audit has passed and the household review is still open. At the temporary reading table Bea prepares separate statements for residents who disagree. Clara faces a question about why she closed the old request. The table rocks. Clara supplies the saucer and Bea lifts the table so it can be placed. Each begins writing her own account.
