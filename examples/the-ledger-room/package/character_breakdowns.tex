\section{Character Breakdowns}
\textbf{Clara Venn,54, woman.} Wants an orderly retirement and the payment that makes her flat affordable. Her lever is the completion signature; her defense is narrowing duty to the document in front of her. She once closed an unanswered query while her permanent post depended on clearing a backlog. The role moves from authority to evasion, humiliating specificity and supervised service. Her final statement exposes a choice rather than purchasing absolution.

\textbf{Jory Senn,37, woman.} A temporary courier who wants permanent hours and a workable college commute for Nell. She bargains in tasks, time and written assurances. Sending the full ledger photograph is a deliberate shortcut. Her late choice is available: ordinary carrier return would preserve the book and release an employment letter, but close her unresolved disclosure incident. She chooses an exception and keeps pursuing wages, shifts and a grievance.

\textbf{Bea Quill,69, woman.} Sewing work, lost rods and a stubborn account of her own move. She can unlock resident knowledge but cannot own resident consent. Ina challenges her habit of protecting people by speaking for them. Bea removes a useful story from the packet and contributes only her own observation.

\textbf{Lida Rusk,48, woman.} Municipal transition director. Wants completed contracts and a defensible flood reserve. She authorized a transfer that can be valid even if household treatment was wrong. She restricts access, offers safer ordinary storage and finally releases narrowly specified records. She supplies evidence without becoming an ally who fixes employment.

\textbf{Ina Werr,44, woman.} Cleaner and unrecognized rent payer. Work gossip has made an abstract inquiry invade her livelihood. She retrieves private rent slips, withholds contact authority and makes Bea surrender representative control. Her refusal changes what travels.

\textbf{Mara Venn,27, woman.} Leaving for a kitchen management job. Refuses to manage Clara's cancellation or become a rescue household. Offers dinner and affection without reversing her departure.

\textbf{Nell Senn,16, girl.} Wants technical training, a room and the workshop trip she has saved for. Negotiates travel money and timing; her mother's job cannot be made her only ambition.

\textbf{Sana Vell,50, woman.} Dispatcher and union delegate with several workers to protect. Rejects an insecure storage favor, supplies limited witness labor and keeps the offers together for a grievance.

\textbf{Mina Olt,43, woman.} Registrar, not adjudicator. Receives precisely declared records and rejects borrowed privacy for Clara's own act.\quad\textbf{Tom Kest,60, man.} Depot worker whose final shift really ends. Witnesses what he can, refuses to invent memory and leaves for his own life.
