\section{Positioning and Comparison Brief}
\textbf{Audience promise.} A character-led institutional drama in which an apparent rescue is complicated by privacy, labor and the rescuer's own record. The procedural satisfaction is specific and modest: evidence is received accurately. The emotional payoff is not compensation or an exposed mastermind.

\textbf{Comparison axes for a producer.} Seek films that sustain work as action; ensemble dramas where supporting people can refuse the lead; contained procedurals with an unresolved social aftermath; adult films with humor inside economic pressure. Compare tone and formal craft separately from admissions or sales. A famous successful title would not establish demand for this project.

\textbf{Differentiation.} Two workers depend on the same signature, then discover that a truthful completion can hurt them differently. The resident who refuses to cooperate changes the evidence packet. The antagonist's flood reserve remains a competing public good rather than a secret fraud pot.

\textbf{Current limit.} This demonstration contains a comparison brief rather than a verified title-and-market comp study. No commercial result, current buyer appetite or comparable budget is asserted. A producer should select and verify actual artistic and financial comparisons after assessing the draft, territory and package. This is preliminary positioning, not market validation.
