\section{Tone, Image and Sound}
The film begins with pleasure in practical competence: a gate turn, a balanced cabinet, paired lifting. Pressure changes the use of that competence. No camera move should announce that paper has become important before the people make it important.

Use ordinary coastal light, worn civic surfaces and working depth. The depot is tall, the counter divides people, the strongroom is small and unimpressive. Keep text legible only when a character must act on it. Details of slips, ticks and seals should alternate with faces deciding whether to use them. Avoid a wall of insert shots masquerading as investigation.

The dominant sounds are labor: trolley wheel, copier feeder, gate bolt, kettle, tape, keys. These sounds acquire new consequences without becoming a symbolic sound catalogue. Music can be withheld at decisions; no triumph cue for the08:53 receipt. Water and floodwall stay present as ordinary infrastructure rather than disaster spectacle.

Dry wit is earned through different needs: Jory makes tasks concrete, Tom keeps boundaries, Bea refuses euphemism, Mara resists delegation. Mina's precision should sound like work, not a philosopher's performance. Ina needs no elegant exit line. Let silences include fatigue, chewing and the time needed to read.

Linear form over forty-eight hours, followed by brief consequences. The final table is smaller than the opening cabinet, and steadied by two people. They remain free to write different accounts.
