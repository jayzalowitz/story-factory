# What a useful story check looks like

These are original teaching examples, not excerpts from another screenplay. Mara and Nina are explicitly women in this fictional character roster. A Bechdel result is separate from whether a scene is any good.

## A scene that does not establish a pass

S03 — INT. LIGHTHOUSE KITCHEN — MORNING

MARA: Did he answer the letter?

NINA: He asked whether you still blame him.

MARA: I only want to know why he left.

NINA: He thinks you know.

**Excerpt result: FAIL.** Two named women address each other, but the entire exchange concerns a particular man. It does not show an independent non-man subject. An excerpt's failure does not establish that the unseen whole film fails.

**Craft problem:** all four lines deliver relationship information. Neither character changes tactics or makes a choice; Nina is a conduit for someone else's feelings. Adding a line about the weather would not repair that dramatic problem.

## A conversation that qualifies and changes the scene

S07 — INT. LIGHTHOUSE KITCHEN — MORNING

On the table: an electricity bill. Nina lays a coastal survey beside it.

NINA: The survey leaves at six. I'm going.

MARA: The last ferry is at five.

NINA: Then I stay overnight.

Mara uncaps a pen and writes a number on the bill.

MARA: That covers your share until Friday.

Nina takes the pen. Crosses out the number. Writes a smaller one.

NINA: It covers until tonight. I don't live here yet.

She takes the survey, leaving the pen.

**Excerpt result: PASS.** Mara and Nina discuss Nina's work, transport, and the financial boundary of their living arrangement. The exchange is not about a man. Evidence: Nina's departure plan, Mara's attempt to extend the arrangement, and Nina's refusal. The character roster supplies identity; names alone would not.

**Why it may be a stronger scene:** Nina has a pursuit outside reconciliation. Mara's practical tactic conceals a wish to keep her. Nina makes a boundary concrete, and the overnight absence changes what Mara can do next. The scene still needs context and a downstream consequence; a test pass does not establish that it belongs in the film.

## An uncertain case

A transcript lists KAI and JO exchanging lines about navigation, but supplies no character identities, no addressee cues, and only two disconnected pages. **UNCLEAR.** Do not guess gender, invent an offscreen conversation, or certify a full screenplay. Ask for the roster/context and state what was actually read.

## An actionable review finding

- Finding: Q-03, major, agency/causality.
- Location: S03, kitchen, draft page 8 if measured.
- Evidence: Nina relays someone else's answer and leaves the situation unchanged.
- Consequence: the daughter functions as exposition; the middle repeats Mara's hurt.
- Proposed repair: give Nina a concrete work decision that conflicts with staying; make Mara respond through action rather than explanation.
- Disposition: rewrite approved by the writer, or proposed only if no approval exists.
- Verification: re-read S07 and the later ferry scene to see whether the decision actually changes the plot.

## A passing test with a weak story

Imagine S07 is followed by twenty pages where Nina waits silently while Mara reads letters. The Bechdel result remains PASS. The character-independence and causal-escalation findings remain REVISE. Story Factory must report both.

The user may choose `--bechdel-target pass` for a new project. The default is `report`: honestly report the result without treating a narrow test as a universal writing rule. In either mode, the report must refer to the current draft.
